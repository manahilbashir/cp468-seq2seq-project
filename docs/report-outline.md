**LLM baseline** — from `llm_baseline.py` and `results/comparison_metrics.json`:

**Results** — from `results/comparison_metrics.json` (generated by `compare_results.py`):
- BLEU + ROUGE-1/2/L for three systems: LSTM, LLM zero-shot, LLM few-shot

The LLM half is already measured on all 270 test examples:

| Prompt setting | BLEU | ROUGE-1 | ROUGE-2 | ROUGE-L |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gemini zero-shot | 3.85 | 0.4536 | 0.2050 | 0.3870 |
| Gemini few-shot (k=3) | 2.64 | 0.4388 | 0.1944 | 0.3758 |

Two things worth a paragraph each:
- **Zero-shot beat few-shot.** This is exactly why the PRD asks for two prompt
  variants. The few-shot exemplars are Western title-case headlines while the
  corpus style is terser, so the examples hurt rather than helped.
- **BLEU is uninformative at this length.** ~10-token outputs with one reference
  collapse BLEU via the brevity penalty and 4-gram precision, while ROUGE-1 of
  0.45 shows the content overlap is actually reasonable. Lead with ROUGE and use
  this as concrete evidence in the "limits of automatic metrics" discussion.

### Required discussion points (PRD §5)

1. **Quantitative gap** — size of the LSTM–LLM gap; check whether it holds across
   article lengths (`qualitative_comparison.md` is already sorted by source length).
2. **Why the gap exists** — capacity, pretraining scale, transformer attention vs.
   recurrent bottleneck, transfer learning. Ground this in our numbers: the LSTM saw
   **2,152 examples**; the LLM saw internet-scale pretraining.
3. **Failure mode contrast** — the PRD explicitly says *verify against your data, do
   not just assert*. `qualitative_comparison.md` flags candidates automatically
   (LSTM repetition/empty output, LLM over-elaboration) but you must read the
   examples and assign the real categories yourself.
4. **Fairness in both directions** — the LLM likely saw this news domain in
   pretraining (contamination risk); our LSTM is tiny, cheap and offline. A fairer
   middle point would be fine-tuning a small pretrained transformer.
5. **Engineering trade-offs** — cost/request, latency, privacy, offline deployment,
   controllability, low-resource domains.
6. **Limitations + ethics** — BLEU/ROUGE reward n-gram overlap, not factuality;
   GPL-2.0 dataset licensing; contamination risk; compute/environmental cost.

### Honest framing note

With only 2,152 training examples the LSTM is **data-starved**, and its scores will
be far below the LLM. That is the expected and intended result — the PRD says
outright *"The goal is not to beat the LLM — you won't."* Report the gap plainly
and explain it; do not oversell the LSTM.
